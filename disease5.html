cold DETAILS

Overview

The common cold is a viral infection of the upper respiratory tract. It is caused by various types of viruses, most commonly rhinoviruses. It is characterized by a runny nose, sore throat, cough, and mild fever. The common cold is highly contagious and can spread through droplets from coughs and sneezes or by touching contaminated surfaces.

Symptoms

Runny or stuffy nose Sore throat Cough Sneezing Mild fever Fatigue Headache Mild body aches

Precautions

Wash hands frequently with soap and water. Use hand sanitizer if soap is not available. Avoid close contact with infected individuals. Cover your mouth and nose with a tissue or elbow when coughing or sneezing. Disinfect commonly touched surfaces. Avoid touching your face, especially your eyes, nose, and mouth.

Causes

The common cold is caused by a variety of viruses, with rhinoviruses being the most common. Other viruses that can cause a cold include: Coronaviruses Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) Parainfluenza viruses Adenoviruses

Medication

There is no cure for the common cold, but symptoms can be managed with: Over-the-counter decongestants Pain relievers (e.g., acetaminophen or ibuprofen) Cough suppressants or expectorants Saline nasal sprays Humidifiers to keep the air moist

When to See a Doctor

Consult a healthcare provider if you experience: High fever (above 101.3°F or 38.5°C) Severe headache Shortness of breath or chest pain Symptoms that worsen or do not improve after 10 days Persistent cough with thick, green, or yellow mucus

Complications

Though rare, complications from a cold can include: Sinusitis (sinus infection) Otitis media (ear infection) Acute bronchitis Exacerbation of asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
