Fever DETAILS

Overview

Fever is a temporary increase in body temperature, often due to an illness. It's a sign that something out of the ordinary is happening in your body. For most children and adults, a fever may be uncomfortable. But it usually isn't a cause for concern unless it reaches 103°F (39.4°C) or higher.

Symptoms

Common symptoms associated with fever include sweating, chills, headache, muscle aches, loss of appetite, irritability, dehydration, and general weakness. If the fever is associated with an infection, symptoms such as cough, sore throat, and vomiting may also occur.

Precautions

To prevent fever, maintain good hygiene, avoid close contact with sick individuals, stay hydrated, get adequate rest, and eat a balanced diet. It’s also essential to wash hands frequently and cover the mouth when coughing or sneezing to reduce the spread of germs.

Causes

Fever is often caused by a viral or bacterial infection, such as the flu, pneumonia, or urinary tract infections. Other causes include heat exhaustion, certain inflammatory conditions, medications, immunizations, and sometimes cancer.

Medication

Fever can often be treated with over-the-counter medications such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen to lower body temperature. It's important to follow the recommended dosages and consult with a healthcare provider if the fever persists or is associated with more serious symptoms.

When to See a Doctor

You should seek medical attention if the fever is 103°F (39.4°C) or higher, lasts more than three days, or is accompanied by severe symptoms like difficulty breathing, chest pain, persistent vomiting, or confusion. Infants under 3 months with a fever should be evaluated by a doctor immediately.

Complications

While most fevers are self-limiting and harmless, a high or prolonged fever can lead to complications such as dehydration, febrile seizures, or more serious conditions like brain damage if not properly managed. It's important to monitor the fever and seek medical advice when necessary.
