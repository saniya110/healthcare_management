Tuberculosis DETAILS

Overview

Tuberculosis (TB) is a serious infectious disease caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It primarily affects the lungs but can also impact other parts of the body. TB is spread through the air when a person with active TB coughs or sneezes. It is treatable with a course of antibiotics, but without treatment, it can be fatal.

Symptoms

Common symptoms of TB include a persistent cough lasting more than three weeks, chest pain, coughing up blood or sputum, weight loss, fever, night sweats, and fatigue. These symptoms can vary in severity and may develop gradually.

Precautions

To prevent TB, individuals should avoid close contact with people who have active TB, maintain good respiratory hygiene (e.g., covering the mouth while coughing), and ensure proper ventilation in living spaces. If you have symptoms or have been exposed to someone with TB, seek medical evaluation promptly.

Causes

Tuberculosis is caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis. The bacteria are transmitted through airborne droplets when an infected person coughs or sneezes. TB can be spread in crowded or poorly ventilated environments, and those with weakened immune systems are at a higher risk of contracting the disease.

Medication

Treatment for tuberculosis involves a combination of antibiotics taken for several months. Commonly prescribed medications include isoniazid, rifampin, ethambutol, and pyrazinamide. It is crucial to complete the full course of treatment to ensure that the infection is fully eradicated and to prevent drug-resistant strains of TB.

When to See a Doctor

Consult a healthcare provider if you experience persistent symptoms such as a prolonged cough, weight loss, or night sweats, or if you have been in contact with someone diagnosed with TB. Early diagnosis and treatment are essential for managing the disease effectively.

Complications

If left untreated, TB can lead to severe complications such as lung damage, respiratory failure, and spread to other parts of the body including the kidneys, spine, and brain. Multi-drug-resistant TB is also a serious complication that requires more intensive treatment.
